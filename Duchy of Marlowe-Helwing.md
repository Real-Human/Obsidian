#### **Overview**

The **Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing** is a major eastern realm, occupying fertile lowlands, river corridors, and the marsh-bound frontier known as the Fenward Marches. Founded as a deliberate instrument of expansion — a ducal wedge driven into unclaimed eastern wilderness — it was once the beating edge of civilisation, where settlers, soldiers, and fortune-seekers pressed outward against unknown lands. In those early centuries, the duchy grew wealthy and proud. Its marshlands yielded coal, peat, and timber; its rivers became arteries of trade; its position as a land bridge between the inner kingdoms and the eastern frontier made it indispensable to the ambitions of every realm to its south and west.

But frontiers do not stand still. As generations passed, the edge of settled land pushed ever further north and east, and with it went the energy, the investment, and the sense of purpose that had once defined Marlowe-Helwing. Trade routes shifted from road to open water. The great coastal ports of neighbouring realms drew commerce away from the old overland corridors — and the cruel irony was not lost on those paying attention. Marlowe-Helwing sits between two separate bodies of water, yet had never built the ships, the ports, or the maritime tradition to compete on the seas that now carried the wealth it had once commanded by land. What had been a prosperous land bridge became, in the eyes of more powerful neighbours, a convenient backwater — too established to be interesting, too weakened to be feared.

Now the frontier has collapsed entirely. The **[[Iron Host]]** has swept through the northern and north-eastern realms that Marlowe-Helwing once helped to settle, sending waves of displaced peoples south and west into the duchy's borders. Mountain barriers have, for now, prevented a direct advance into the Fenward Marches, but the pressure on northern routes and crossings mounts with each season. The duchy that once looked outward with ambition now looks north with dread — and finds itself ill-prepared for what it sees.

At the heart of this crisis is the prolonged illness of **[[Duke Helwing]]**, which has left central authority paralysed at the worst possible moment. With no definitive voice calling the duchy to arms, Marlowe-Helwing drifts. Town councils wield unprecedented power. Emergency decrees go unchallenged. Regional strongmen fill the vacuum. A realm that was already a shadow of its former self is, by degrees, sleepwalking toward extinction.

#### **Capital and Seat of Power**

##### **On the Distinction Between Capital and Ducal Seat**

Though often referred to as a single "capital" in casual speech, Marlowe-Helwing maintains a long-standing division between **legal authority** and **practical governance** — a division that now cuts to the heart of the duchy's dysfunction.

- **[[Helwing Hold]]** is the _ducal seat_: the site of the Duke's court, high law, and the duchy's archives. It is from here that authority formally emanates, and it is here that the Duke lies bedridden, unreachable by petition and incapable of decree.
    
- **[[Highford]]** is the _principal settlement_: the largest city and the economic, logistical, and political heart of the duchy. Most decisions that affect daily life are now made — or simply enacted without sanction — in Highford.
    

This division has existed for generations, a relic of an older model of governance in which the Duke's court projected authority outward rather than embedding itself in commerce. In prosperous times, it was merely an inconvenience. In the present crisis, it has become a structural wound.

#### **Geography**

The duchy spans three broad zones:

- **The Fenward Marches** — eastern marshlands, peat flats, and deep coal seams
- **The Helwing Lowlands** — arable farmland, manors, and market towns
- **The Western Ridges** — higher ground, old stone roads, and fortified crossings

What is less often remarked upon — and rarely appreciated by those who have not studied its shape — is that the duchy is flanked by open water on both sides. A separate body of water lies along the western coast, beyond the Western Ridges, and another borders the eastern edge of the Fenward Marches. Neither has been meaningfully developed. Small fishing settlements dot both coastlines, sustaining themselves on modest catches and local trade, but the duchy has no ports of consequence, no merchant fleet, and no naval tradition. The sea is a feature of the landscape rather than a resource — which goes some way toward explaining why Marlowe-Helwing watched helplessly as maritime commerce bypassed it entirely.

River travel dominates trade and communication, binding the duchy together even as its political unity frays.

The northern boundary is defined by broken highlands and narrow passes descending from [[Iron Host]]-controlled territory. These routes are difficult to traverse in force but impossible to ignore, and northern crossings and river choke-points have become the focus of what military planning the duchy can still muster. Eastern settlements in the Fenward Marches remain comparatively insulated from immediate assault — though those who remember the duchy's founding understand that "insulated" is not the same as "safe".

#### **Major Settlements**

##### **[[Highford]]**

**Principal Settlement of the Duchy**

- **Population:** ~14,000
- **Geographical Position:** Central-southern Marlowe-Helwing, where the duchy's main river bends and broadens at its principal stone bridge
- **Role:** Trade hub, river crossing, customs and grain market
- **Notes:** Highford sits at the safest and most reliable crossing of the duchy's main river artery, where east–west river traffic meets the north–south road network. Its position places it well clear of the marshlands whilst still controlling access to the [[Fenward Marches]] beyond. Though diminished from its peak as a continental trade hub, it remains the most populous and practically influential settlement in the duchy, and in the absence of ducal leadership it has become the de facto seat of political power.

---

##### **[[Helwing Hold]]**

**Seat of the Duke**

- **Population:** ~5,000
- **Geographical Position:** [[Western Ridges]], west of [[Highford]], overlooking the old stone road into the inner duchies
- **Role:** Ducal residence, high courts, archives, noble estates
- **Notes:** Built on defensible high ground, Helwing Hold commands road access rather than river trade. It reflects an older vision of the duchy — a stronghold from which authority was projected outward into frontier lands. With the Duke incapacitated, the Hold has taken on the quality of a monument to that older order: formally supreme, practically inert. Its archives hold ducal charters, border agreements, and early frontier compacts that have not been opened in a generation.

---

##### **[[Redmarsh]]**

- **Population:** ~2,000
- **Geographical Position:** Eastern [[Fenward Marches]], on a low ridge where the marsh gives way to stable ground beside a navigable tributary
- **Role:** Coal extraction, river barging, industrial workshops
- **Notes:** The easternmost major settlement under firm ducal authority — what little such authority still means — Redmarsh's ridge location keeps it above seasonal flooding whilst allowing direct access to marsh-bound coal seams and river transport. It is also the site of the duchy's most consequential industrial secret: the deep seams here hold significant deposits of **[[Wondrous Coal]]**, a volatile and poorly understood magical mineral whose true value is known only to a handful of individuals.

---

##### **[[Grayfen Crossing]]**

- **Population:** ~6,500
- **Geographical Position:** North-eastern duchy, where the fen-rivers narrow into a controlled crossing
- **Role:** Toll collection, ferries, patrol mustering
- **Notes:** Grayfen Crossing controls one of the few reliable routes through the northern marshes, making it a natural choke-point and the site of the duchy's heaviest fortification. It is one of the settlements most directly exposed to refugee pressure from the north, and its garrison has been stretched thin managing both incoming displaced peoples and the ever-present risk of Iron Host scouts probing the passes.

---

##### **[[Brackenmere]]**

- **Population:** ~5,000
- **Geographical Position:** Northern Fenward Marches, east of the central river spine, on a sluggish river near the marsh–upland transition
- **Role:** Peat cutting, reed harvest, fen livestock, frontier supply
- **Notes:** Brackenmere sits on precarious ground where raised causeways and semi-dry banks allow permanent settlement amid the Fenward marshes. It relies on peat, reeds, and hardy marsh livestock, exporting fuel and fodder westward whilst serving as a supply and staging point for those moving deeper into the Marches. Flooding and seasonal isolation shape its population: pragmatic, insular, and long accustomed to the idea that the duchy's centre cannot be relied upon.

##### **Other Towns & Holdings**

###### **Stonecross**

- **Population:** ~4,000
- **Geographical Position:** Northern Lowlands, well north of [[Highford]], at a major stone bridge where the primary north–south road crosses the main river
- **Role:** Bridge control, toll collection, garrison town, troop mustering
- **Notes:** Stonecross commands one of the most critical permanent crossings in the duchy, sitting on the main road that links the southern Lowlands to the northern frontier. Any movement of force or trade between Highford and the northern approaches inevitably passes through it, making it both heavily policed and politically sensitive. Its garrison represents one of the largest concentrations of standing ducal troops remaining in the field.

###### **Willowreach**

- **Population:** ~3,500
- **Geographical Position:** Southern Lowlands, downstream of [[Highford]] along a broad, slow-moving river reach
- **Role:** River trade, fishing, reed harvesting, barge construction and maintenance
- **Notes:** Willowreach lies along a gentle bend of the main river, its economy tied closely to seasonal river conditions and the bulk trade moving southward — grain, timber, and peat from upriver settlements. It is stable rather than strategic, and largely removed from the duchy's current crises, though its barge-builders and river captains have noticed a marked decline in traffic over the past decade as sea routes draw commerce away from inland waterways.

###### **Villages & Hamlets**

- **Population:** ~30,000 combined
- **Geographical Position:** Scattered throughout the Helwing Lowlands, along minor river tributaries, and in small clusters along both the western and eastern coastlines
- **Notes:** Many have absorbed refugees or been abandoned entirely, depending on proximity to marshland and trade routes. The villages speak most plainly to the duchy's long decline: once prosperous agricultural communities supplying a busy overland trade corridor, many are now quiet, half-emptied, or struggling under the weight of displaced peoples they can barely feed. The coastal settlements are a modest exception — small fishing hamlets on both coasts that have never been prosperous enough to attract much attention, and are consequently among the most self-sufficient and least politically entangled communities in the duchy.

##### **Ancestry Breakdown (Approx.)**

The duchy's founding as a frontier settlement drew a wide mix of peoples, and centuries of trade, migration, and refugee movement have only broadened that diversity. The following reflects the established population; refugee communities skew this considerably in affected settlements.

**Common Ancestries**

- **Humans** (~40%) — The plurality ancestry across all regions and social strata. Most noble houses, town councils, and ducal institutions are human-dominated, though this reflects political entrenchment as much as raw numbers.
- **Dwarves** (~12%) — A strong and long-established presence, particularly in the Fenward Marches and mining settlements. Many dwarven families have been in Marlowe-Helwing for generations, drawn originally by the coal seams. They are disproportionately represented among miners, engineers, and craft guilds.
- **Orcs** (~9%) — Well-established in the northern Lowlands and the frontier-adjacent communities, where their ancestors settled as soldiers and labourers during the duchy's expansionist centuries. Orc communities tend toward insularity and self-reliance, having learned not to expect much from ducal authority. Refugee orcs from the northern freeholds have swelled existing communities considerably.
- **Halflings** (~7%) — Common throughout the Lowlands and river settlements, where they cluster in trade, domestic service, and river work. Halfling communities are among the most mobile in the duchy and often have the clearest picture of conditions across multiple settlements, making them valuable — and frequently underestimated — sources of information.
- **Gnomes** (~5%) — Present in modest numbers, particularly in towns with active craft or artificer traditions. A small but notable gnome community has long been associated with [[Redmarsh]]'s industrial trades.

**Uncommon Ancestries**

- **Elves** (~4%) — Mostly wood and marsh elves in the Fenward Marches and Western Ridge forests, living in semi-isolated communities with limited integration into ducal politics. Their long memory of the duchy's better days gives them a particular wariness about its present trajectory.
- **Goblins** (~3%) — Found in river settlements and the rougher quarters of larger towns, where they fill roles in salvage, waste management, and light engineering. Regarded with varying degrees of suspicion depending on the settlement; Highford's goblin population is well-integrated, while smaller communities are less welcoming.
- **Lizardfolk** (~2%) — A quiet but established presence in the deeper Fenward Marches, where their affinity for wetland terrain makes them natural inhabitants. They largely govern themselves through their own community structures and engage with ducal institutions on their own terms — which is to say, rarely.
- **Catfolk** (~2%) — Scattered throughout trade settlements and river towns, typically as merchants, travellers, or those who arrived with trade caravans and stayed. No significant concentrated community exists, but individual catfolk are common enough to attract little attention in most towns.
- **Kobolds** (~1%) — Small enclaves exist near the mining districts, where kobold skill in tunnel-craft and underground navigation has made them useful, if not always respected, members of mining communities.

**Versatile Heritages**

- **Nephilim** — Rare but present, and notably disproportionate near the older frontier settlements of the north, where the duchy's history of conflict and hardship has occasionally drawn the attention of outer-planar entities. Both empyrean and cambion-lineaged individuals are known, and neither is treated with particular reverence or hostility in most parts of the duchy — they are simply an uncommon fact of life. A small number hold positions of local authority, their otherworldly aspect lending them a degree of gravity that purely mortal politicians sometimes lack.
- **Dhampir** — Quietly present, particularly in the older noble families of the Western Ridges, where the long history of the duchy has occasionally produced entanglements of the sort that leave their mark across generations. Not openly acknowledged in polite society, but not unknown.

**Refugee Population Note** The ~20,000–25,000 refugees who have entered the duchy from the north skew heavily towards humans, orcs, and mixed-ancestry folk. They have strained infrastructure in the Lowlands significantly and introduced small numbers of ancestries less common to Marlowe-Helwing — including scattered groups of ratfolk and hobgoblins from fallen frontier militias — whose reception has been mixed at best.

#### **Military Forces**

**Total available forces:** ~9,000–11,000

- Standing ducal troops: ~3,500
- Town militias: ~4,000
- River wardens & patrols: ~1,500
- Noble retainers & private forces: ~1,000

Command is deeply fragmented. Loyalty is owed to towns, local patrons, or employers far more reliably than to the duchy as a whole. The **[[Displaced Companies]]** — veteran soldiers from fallen northern realms operating within and beyond the duchy's borders — represent a significant irregular force that answers to no ducal authority, a complication that is simultaneously a potential resource and a source of considerable anxiety for those who still hold formal power.

#### **Governance & Current State**

The duchy's governance was already weakened by decades of economic decline before the Duke's illness made that weakness catastrophic. With no central authority capable of issuing binding orders or enforcing ducal law:

- Emergency powers persist at the local level without clear oversight or expiry
- Town assemblies wield unprecedented authority, often in direct competition with one another
- Economic and industrial interests increasingly shape policy in the absence of political leadership
- Noble houses have retreated into private manoeuvring rather than collective action
- The Iron Host threat goes formally unaddressed, with no coordinated military response in preparation

Marlowe-Helwing remains wealthy enough to defend itself, populous enough to field a meaningful army, and geographically positioned well enough to make a stand — but it possesses none of the political cohesion required to do any of these things. The duchy is not yet broken. It is simply waiting, without knowing it is waiting, for something to break it.
