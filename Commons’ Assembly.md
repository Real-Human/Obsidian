**Type:** Civil representative council **Location:** [[Redmarsh]], [[Fenward Marches]] **Leadership:** [[Marta Goodwin]] (Speaker) **Membership:** ~24 elected delegates from mining wards, trade quarters, and outlying villages **Status:** Functioning, but under sustained and deliberate pressure from [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office

#### **Overview**

The **Commons' Assembly** is the elected civil council of [[Redmarsh]], representing the town's mining wards, trades, river workers, and the surrounding marshland villages. It is the only body in [[Redmarsh]] that draws its authority directly from the public, and the only institution still capable, in principle, of questioning the mayor.

In practice, that capability is being eroded session by session.

[[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s emergency powers were never meant to last. They were granted as a temporary measure during a leasehold crisis and a flood year, and they were always supposed to bend back to the Assembly once the crisis had passed. The crisis has not been allowed to pass. With each delayed report, each "lost" petition, each clerk reassigned and each licence quietly revoked, [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office strangles the Assembly's authority a little further. The body still meets, still debates, still issues findings — but the levers it once pulled are increasingly disconnected from anything beyond the Chambers' walls.

The Assembly does not yet recognise the full scale of the squeeze. [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]] does. She holds the chamber together by sheer competence, but she knows what kind of position she is defending: a body that, at its current rate, will be a ceremonial shell within a year or two unless something fundamental shifts.

What she does not know — and what no delegate suspects — is that the truest source of [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s position is not his patronage or his paperwork, but a substance the Assembly has been told is industrial waste: [[Wondrous Coal]].

#### **Origins and Tradition**

The Assembly is older than the mines that now dominate it. Like the **town reeves**, **mining councils**, and **barge wardens** found across the [[Fenward Marches]], it descends from the marsh-country tradition of communal self-governance — pragmatic bodies grown out of small, isolated populations where survival required cooperation and disputes could not wait for distant ducal courts.

In its earliest form, the Assembly was little more than a weekly gathering of pithead foremen, ridge-farmers, and barge captains arguing under a tarpaulin in the original market square. Formal records, elected wards, and the office of Speaker came piecemeal across generations, in response to particular disasters, scandals, or charters granted by passing ducal officials. Its powers were never granted in a single document; they accumulated.

This origin matters. The Assembly's authority rests on **custom and continuity**, not on a single legible charter. That makes it resilient against direct attack — no one document can be revoked to abolish it — but uniquely vulnerable to slow erosion. [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s methods exploit this perfectly: he does not challenge the Assembly's right to exist, only its right to act in any specific case.

#### **Powers and Limits**

By long-standing custom, the Assembly holds nominal authority over:

- **Labour standards** in the mines and on the docks (working hours, rest, tool issue, safety inspections)
- **Mine charters and renewals** for any operation drawing on common ridge or fenland
- **Local taxation and tolls**, including barge dues, market fees, and ward levies
- **Civic infrastructure** — roads, bridges, causeway maintenance, the burial fund
- **Public order matters** below the threshold of ducal law (market disputes, licensing, minor sentencing)

In **theory**, almost nothing in [[Redmarsh]]'s daily life is supposed to happen without the Assembly's stamp.

In **practice**, [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s emergency powers — granted by the ducal court during the leasehold crisis and never formally rescinded — overlap with nearly every one of these areas. Where the two authorities collide, [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office wins, not by law, but by the simple fact that he commands the watchmen at the causeway gates, the clerks who stamp the permits, and the foremen who close the cages. The Assembly may declare a mine unsafe; only [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] can stop the cage going down.

The Assembly retains three powers [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] has not been able to neutralise:

1. **Public Hearing.** Sessions are open. Anyone may attend, and findings are spoken aloud in front of witnesses. [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] cannot suppress a debate that has already happened in front of two hundred miners.
2. **Petition.** Any delegate may bring a matter to the floor. While the mayor's clerks "lose" individual petitions on their way upstairs, they cannot prevent a motion being raised in the chamber itself.
3. **Inquiry.** The Assembly may, by majority vote, appoint delegates to investigate specific matters. These inquiries lack enforcement powers but may publish findings — and a published finding is hard to bury.

Each of these is a thin wire. None is enough alone. Together, they are the reason [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] still bothers to attend.

#### **Composition**

The Assembly seats **roughly two dozen delegates**, drawn from four broad categories:

- **Mining wards** — the largest bloc, with delegates elected by pithead voting amongst registered miners. Each major shaft and seam complex sends at least one representative.
- **Trades and crafts** — delegates from the river-haulers' guild, the carters, the smithies, the lamp-glass workshops, and the smaller mills.
- **Outlying villages** — envoys sent by the marshland hamlets and ridge farms within [[Redmarsh]]'s economic orbit.
- **Guild and workshop seats** — a smaller number of seats reserved for established workshops and millers, including a non-voting observer's chair currently held by the [[Glasswrights' Covenant|Glasswrights]].

Elections are staggered, with roughly a third of seats contested each year. The franchise is broader than is typical for towns of this size — any registered worker, householder, or guild member of standing may vote — but registration itself has become a quiet battleground. A worker who falls behind on company-terrace rent, for example, is liable to find their name "pending review" come the next election.

The Speaker is elected from amongst sitting delegates by simple majority and serves until removed or replaced. The position carries no formal additional powers, only the right to set the agenda and chair the floor — which, in practice, is enough.

#### **The Chambers and Sessions**

The Assembly Chambers stand a short walk from the Town Square in [[Redmarsh]], a sturdy stone-and-timber hall reinforced over generations rather than ever properly rebuilt. The benches form a flattened half-circle facing the Speaker's desk; the public stand or sit on the perimeter, and on busy days they crowd the doorway and spill out onto the steps. The acoustics are poor and the chimney smokes, both deliberately preserved by tradition. Heat in winter comes from a single iron stove and the press of bodies.

A session begins with the **Roll of Standing** — the Speaker's clerk reading the names of every delegate present and noting absences. This is partly tradition and partly a practical defence: it is harder for [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office to claim a meeting was unrepresentative when the names of every attendee are on public record.

Debate is direct, often interrupted, and rarely formal in tone. Motions are raised, seconded, and voted on by **show of hand**, with closer votes recounted by **standing division**. A simple majority carries most measures; matters of charter, taxation, or formal inquiry require a **two-thirds vote**. Sessions end with the **Reading Out** — findings, decisions, and any open petitions read aloud at the doorway so that those who could not enter still hear the result.

[[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] attends in person when summoned, often accompanied by his solicitor and a clerk carrying a leather case of figures. He never raises his voice. He answers every question politely, with reference to documentation, and leaves before the Reading Out so that the final word is not in his ear.

#### **Speaker: Marta Goodwin**

[[Marta Goodwin]] holds the Speakership in her third elected term, having first taken the chair after her predecessor stepped down following a contested vote on mine inspections. Her style is detailed in her own dossier; what matters for the Assembly is what her presence makes possible.

She has the rare combination of **moral authority and procedural fluency**. Where another Speaker might be drawn into open confrontation with [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] — which she would lose — she instead asks for figures, sets them against earlier figures, requests clarification, and tables motions of inquiry. She has yet to defeat [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] on a single major issue. She has also never given him the clean, dismissive victory he wants.

She is the principal reason the Assembly remains a meaningful body. She is also tired in a way she does not show.

#### **Voices in the Chamber**

The Assembly's politics break along three rough tendencies — **caution**, **ambition**, and **pragmatism** — described in fuller form at the end of this entry. None of these is an organised faction; delegates do not gather under banners or coordinate strategy in advance. They are patterns of instinct, predictable from session to session but not absolute. A delegate may vote with one tendency on safety and another on taxation; alliances shift case by case.

Three figures, beyond the Speaker, exemplify these tendencies and are amongst the most active voices in the chamber.

##### **Hessen Brack**

_Half-orc; pithead delegate (Eastvein ward); welfare-focused_

Hessen took the Eastvein delegate's seat after a roof-fall killed seventeen of his work-gang, including the previous delegate. He had been a foreman for twelve years; he is now a widower, and he does not speak about either of those facts in the chamber. He is broad-shouldered and softly spoken, and the silence before he answers a question makes seasoned solicitors hesitate.

He is [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]]'s most reliable ally on matters of mine safety, working hours, and the burial fund. His weakness is patience — he will hold his temper through a great deal, but the things he loses it over tend to be the things [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office most wants quietly tabled. [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]] has, on more than one occasion, called a recess to let him walk it off.

He carries a small notebook in which he has been writing the names of every miner killed in the deeper shafts since he took his seat. He has not shown it to anyone. He has not yet asked himself what he intends to do with it.

##### **Calla Westren**

_Human; trades delegate (Dockside Quarter); growth-aligned_

Calla arrived in [[Redmarsh]] from [[Highford]] eight years ago with capital and a clear plan. She owns two of the larger supply concerns in the Dockside Quarter and holds her seat with the comfortable backing of the carters and shopkeepers her business sustains. She is articulate, well-dressed by [[Redmarsh]] standards, and unfailingly civil to opponents she intends to outlast.

She is not on [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s payroll, and she would be offended by the suggestion. She is, however, a true believer in what she calls **modernisation** — and her model of modernisation looks remarkably like whatever [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] proposed last week. She frames safety motions as sentiment, inquiry motions as obstruction, and [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]]'s caution as the reflex of an older generation that does not understand what [[Redmarsh]] is becoming.

When she opposes [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] — and she does, occasionally, on tax burdens that fall on her own merchants — she does so with such evident reluctance that her ordinary support reads as principled rather than purchased. This is the trick of her, and it is a good one.

##### **Brisa Dunlow**

_Halfling; river-haulers' delegate; pragmatist_

Brisa is the third generation of Dunlows to captain barges on the Fenward River, and the first to hold a seat in the Chambers. She represents the river-haulers but understands the mines, the farms, and the carters with equal clarity, because all of them are eventually her cargo. She speaks plainly, swears more than the chair encourages, and treats every motion as a logistical problem before she treats it as a political one.

She is the figure both [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]] and [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] try to court, because she is genuinely undecided on the larger question. She thinks [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s checkpoints are a curse on her trade and his road repairs a blessing. She thinks [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]] is right about the deep shafts and naïve about the speed at which [[Redmarsh]] needs to grow if it is to survive whatever is moving south from the [[Iron Host]]. She votes case by case, and her vote often carries the swing seats with her.

If a session goes [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s way, it is usually because Brisa has decided this particular fight is not worth her river. If a session goes [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]]'s way, it is often because Brisa has decided that this particular silence would cost more than the row.

#### **The Slow Suffocation**

The Assembly is not being attacked. It is being **starved**.

[[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] understood early that a frontal assault on a body with custom and public sympathy on its side would only generate the martyrdom he could not afford. Instead, his office has spent years quietly reshaping the conditions in which the Assembly operates, in ways no single session can address.

- **Procedural delay.** Reports requested by Assembly inquiry arrive late, incomplete, or with inconvenient figures buried in technical addenda. By the time they can be properly read, the next session has overtaken them.
- **Petition loss.** Petitions submitted to the mayor's office for action are "received" but not acted upon. The clerks responsible are unfailingly apologetic.
- **Selective enforcement.** Assembly findings that align with [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s interests are enforced briskly. Those that do not are lost in the queue of more pressing matters.
- **Registration friction.** Voters whose politics inconvenience the mayor's office find themselves "pending review" at election time. Most resolve before the next election. Some do not.
- **Patronage.** Permits, licences, and contracts flow disproportionately to delegates who are easy to work with — and dry up around those who are not. Few delegates are bought outright. Many are simply rewarded or punished, often without realising which is happening.
- **Information asymmetry.** [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] arrives at every session with detailed, polished figures. The Assembly has no comparable apparatus and cannot easily challenge his numbers in the moment.
- **Time.** Sessions are held only fortnightly. Crises require action in days. By the time the chamber convenes, [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office has already done what it intended to do.

Each of these is small. None is, on its own, a scandal. Together, they constitute the slow conversion of a working civic body into a discussion forum for decisions made elsewhere.

[[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]] sees the pattern. So, in their separate ways, do Hessen Brack and Brisa Dunlow. None of them yet has the tool to break it.

#### **Active Pressures and Debates**

Several matters dominate current sessions. None is close to resolution.

##### **The Deep Shaft Question**

Mortality in the deeper shafts has risen significantly over the past two years. [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office attributes this to **unusual geological instability**, supported by detailed, plausible figures. The Assembly has accepted this explanation — for now — and the debate has shifted to compensation, ventilation, and rest hours rather than the underlying cause.

The Assembly does not know that the instability is the presence of [[Wondrous Coal]] and the hazardous techniques required to extract it. It debates the symptoms in good faith, unaware that the disease has been concealed.

##### **Volatile Refuse Disposal**

[[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] reports the careful destruction of unstable mineral refuse drawn from the deeper shafts. The Assembly regards this as sensible. They have asked for inspection rights once and been politely refused; they have not yet pressed the point. A delegate who did press it would be raising the most dangerous question in [[Redmarsh]] without realising what they were asking.

##### **The Glasswrights' Expanding Role**

The [[Glasswrights' Covenant|Glasswrights]]' contracts for safe-lamps, ventilators, and winding engines have grown substantially. The Assembly views this as a sensible response to mining hazards. Mild concern has been raised about the guild's privileged access to mine sites and the unusual frequency of closed-door meetings between [[Master Sola Meinh]] and the mayor's office. Nothing has come of these concerns.

##### **Restoration of Regular Governance**

The longest-running debate concerns the sunsetting of [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s special powers and the return of charter authority to the Assembly. [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] has not refused this. He has, instead, agreed in principle on every occasion the matter is raised, and produced a list of remaining crises that must first be resolved. The list has not grown shorter.

##### **The Refugee Question**

Displaced peoples from northern duchies have begun to filter into [[Redmarsh]] in small numbers. [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s office has been generous with shelter and stingy with registration, producing a growing population of unenfranchised workers whose labour benefits the mines and whose votes do not exist. The Assembly has noticed. It has not yet decided what to do about it.

#### **Pressure Points and Hooks**

For a GM running political play in [[Redmarsh]], the Assembly is at its most useful as a body that _could_ still act, but does not yet have the lever. The structure is laid out so that any of the following could shift it decisively.

- **A document.** [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s **Black Book** or the **Deed Codicil** reaching the Speaker's desk would not destroy him by itself, but would give [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]] the basis for a formal inquiry he could not procedurally bury.
- **A specimen.** A single piece of stabilised [[Wondrous Coal]] in the chamber, with someone in attendance who could explain what it is, would crack open the entire concealed economy at once.
- **A witness.** A foreman, [[Glasswrights' Covenant|Glasswrights]] apprentice, or barge captain willing to testify under public hearing about what they have actually seen below.
- **A defection.** Calla Westren turning against [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] — over a tax issue, a personal slight, or a moral line she did not realise she had — would not change the Assembly's votes much, but would change the air in the chamber overnight.
- **A death.** The next major collapse in the deep shafts, if it claims enough lives or the wrong family, may push Hessen Brack past the point at which procedure restrains him.
- **A flood.** A serious flood season would expose the limits of [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s emergency competence and force the Assembly to coordinate relief in ways that re-establish its practical authority.

The body is doomed at its current trajectory. It is not yet too late.

#### **Internal Tendencies**

The Assembly has no formal factions. Delegates do not coordinate under shared banners, and most would deny belonging to any grouping at all. Three loose tendencies nonetheless shape its debates.

**Caution.** Welfare-focused delegates, often from mining wards or smaller villages, instinctively favour safety, transparency, and the slowing of any process they cannot scrutinise. They are [[Marta Goodwin|Goodwin]]'s natural support. Hessen Brack is the clearest current example. The tendency's strength is moral authority; its weakness is that it is reactive, defending against changes already underway.

**Ambition.** Delegates aligned with rapid growth, expanded output, and easy cooperation with the mayor's office. They tend to hold trade or workshop seats and benefit, directly or indirectly, from [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s patronage. They do not consider themselves [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s creatures and would object to the description; they see themselves as realists championing [[Redmarsh]]'s future. Calla Westren is their most articulate voice. The tendency's strength is procedural fluency and access to information; its weakness is its dependence on [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s continued ascendancy.

**Pragmatism.** The largest and least visible tendency, made up of older, technically minded delegates whose chief concern is keeping [[Redmarsh]] functioning. They are open to expansion but wary of risk, sympathetic to safety but impatient with sentiment, and generally trusted across the chamber precisely because they will not pick fights for ideological reasons. Brisa Dunlow exemplifies the disposition. They are the swing voices. Whichever tendency holds them on a given motion holds the chamber.

These instincts cross-cut one another and shift case by case. A safety motion may unite caution and pragmatism against ambition; a tax motion may align ambition and pragmatism against caution; a procedural motion on [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s emergency powers may divide all three. No delegate is wholly predictable. That unpredictability, more than any organised resistance, is what keeps the chamber alive.
