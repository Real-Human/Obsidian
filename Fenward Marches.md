_Eastern Region of the [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing]]_

#### **Overview**

The **Fenward Marches** form the marsh-bound eastern reach of the [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing]]: a low, water-logged country of peat flats, reed beds, and slow black rivers, broken only by scattered ridges of firmer ground where settlement is possible. The region was the duchy's original frontier — the ragged edge of cultivated land, beyond which lay the untamed north and the open eastern waters. Many of its older settlements grew not from planning but from necessity, taking root wherever the ground would bear weight and the water would carry a barge.

The Marches are economically significant out of all proportion to their population. Coal seams run deep beneath the ridges, and the eastern fens hold the richest known deposits of **[[Wondrous Coal]]** — a rare and unstable mineral whose true value is known only to a select few. Publicly, it is dismissed as a hazard. Privately, it has begun to reshape the politics of the entire region.

In recent years the Marches have come under new pressure. The conflict to the north has ended in defeat for the duchies, kingdoms, and earldoms that once shielded the frontier, and refugees now press south through the highland passes. The **[[Iron Host]]** has not yet crossed into the duchy, nor has war been formally declared — but the mountains north of the Marches are no longer a buffer. They are a delay.

#### **Founding and Character**

The Marches were settled piecemeal, in fits and starts, by communities chasing peat, fish, ore, or simply ground that would not flood. Few of the older towns were _planned_ in any meaningful sense; they grew where conditions allowed and stopped growing where conditions did not. **[[Brackenmere]]** is the clearest example — once the foremost town of the eastern fens, now stagnant and visibly worn, a settlement that long ago outgrew the assumptions it was built on.

This piecemeal heritage gives the Marches a particular character. The people here are pragmatic, insular, and slow to trust outsiders, but quick to trust competence. Authority is regarded with mild scepticism, ceremony with open contempt, and weather with the seriousness others reserve for war. Communities cooperate when the rivers rise and argue stubbornly the rest of the year. A reeve who can plan a causeway repair and settle a fishing dispute in the same afternoon will keep her seat indefinitely; one who cannot will not last a season.

The duchy's wealthier districts regard the Marches as backward, damp, and faintly embarrassing. The Marches return the compliment by paying the lowest practical taxes, tolerating ducal officials only as far as the nearest ferry, and quietly running their own affairs.

#### **Geography**

The Marches occupy the broad eastern lobe of the duchy. Their northern boundary is the highland barrier separating the duchy from the territories now under [[Iron Host]] control; their eastern boundary is the open water of the duchy's coast; and their southern boundary runs roughly level with [[Highford]], beyond which the land rises into the well-drained fields of the [[Helwing Lowlands]].

##### **The Fens**

Vast wetlands of reeds, peat, and slow blackwater channels, blanketed for much of the year in low mist. Travel is possible along established barge routes and raised causeways, but routes shift with seasonal flooding and a path reliable in spring may be impassable by autumn. Local guides — barge families, peat-cutters, fen-wardens — know the safe channels; outsiders frequently do not, and the Marches absorb the consequences.

##### **The Ridges**

Narrow spines of compact earth rising a few metres above the fen, often only wide enough for a single road or a row of buildings. These provide the only reliable building sites in the Marches and serve as natural overland routes between settlements. **[[Redmarsh]]** sits on one of the most prominent of these ridges; the lesser hamlets of the region cluster on smaller ones.

##### **The Eastern Coast**

The Marches end at open water. Most of the coastline is unsuitable for serious settlement: salt-crusted reed flats, treacherous mudbanks, silted river mouths, and shifting tidal channels that can be a barge route one season and a trap the next. The duchy has never developed a true port on this coast, and what coastal communities exist are small, scattered, and overwhelmingly concerned with fishing, fowling, and salt-cutting rather than wider trade. Whatever lies beyond the water is, for most folk in the Marches, a matter of rumour rather than experience.

This is not to say the coast is irrelevant. Boats _do_ come and go; smugglers work the more navigable inlets; and the duchy's eastward gaze, such as it is, runs through the Marches whether the Marches like it or not. But no major settlement looks seaward, and the coast functions in practice as a quiet, sparsely-watched edge.

##### **The Northern Approaches**

The broken highlands forming the duchy's northern border lie just beyond the Marches' upper edge. The passes are narrow, difficult to traverse in force, and easier to defend than to assault — a fact that has so far prevented direct invasion. They are not, however, impassable to determined groups in small numbers, and refugees, scouts, and worse have all made the crossing in recent months.

The climate throughout is damp and cool. Summers are short and biting with insects; winters are long, wet, and prone to fog so thick that travel halts entirely for days at a time. The coast adds its own weather: sea fog in spring and autumn, hard easterly storms in winter, and a salt-tang carried inland on bad days as far as Brackenmere.

#### **Watershed**

Two distinct river systems drain the Marches, and the distinction matters more than most outsiders realise.

The first system feeds the **Highwater River** through two principal tributaries — one passing through **[[Grayfen Crossing]]** at the Lowlands–Marches border, the other running south through **[[Redmarsh]]** before joining the Highwater near [[Highford]]. Goods, news, and people moving along these tributaries reach the wider duchy with relative ease.

The second system drains the northern fens past **[[Brackenmere]]** and east toward the coast. In principle this might offer Brackenmere a sea route; in practice it does not. The river's lower reaches have silted heavily over generations, and what was once a navigable mouth is now a maze of shifting channels, sandbars, and reed flats fit only for shallow fishing skiffs. No barge of useful size can make the passage, and no trader has seriously attempted to clear it in living memory. The result is that Brackenmere is functionally cut off from both the Highwater system and the open sea — a town with two coasts and access to neither.

To reach Brackenmere from Redmarsh requires a long overland journey by causeway and marsh road, and to reach it from anywhere else generally requires going through Redmarsh first. This isolation is a substantial part of why Brackenmere has stagnated while Redmarsh has grown.

#### **Major Settlements**

##### **[[Brackenmere]]**

**Population:** ~5,000  
**Position:** Northern Fenward Marches, on a sluggish river east of the central river spine  
**Role:** Peat cutting, reed harvest, fen livestock, frontier supply

The oldest substantial settlement of the Marches, [[Brackenmere]] was never intended to become a town. It began as a cluster of peat-cutters' camps on the only stable banks for miles, and grew because nowhere better existed within easy reach. For generations it was the foremost settlement of the eastern fens — but the assumptions that made it viable have eroded faster than the ground itself. Its river leads nowhere useful; its causeways need yearly repair the duchy increasingly fails to fund; and the marsh has been creeping inward across what were once productive cuttings.

The town today is large but visibly worn. Buildings lean into one another for support. Reed-thatch is patched with reed-thatch. The market is half what it was a century ago, and the wealthier families have either moved west or hold property in [[Highford]] they rarely visit. What remains is a population accustomed to making do, deeply rooted, and quietly proud of having outlasted better-built places. Brackenmere is not dying — it has simply stopped growing, and the difference is visible in every street.

##### **[[Redmarsh]]**

**Population:** ~2,000  
**Position:** Eastern Marches, on a long ridge above a navigable Highwater tributary  
**Role:** Coal extraction, river barging, industrial workshops; covertly, [[Wondrous Coal]] extraction

[[Redmarsh]] is the youngest and most rapidly growing of the Marches' major settlements. Where [[Brackenmere]] grew by accident, Redmarsh has grown by design — specifically, by the design of **[[Ephraim Gauge]]**, whose emergency mayoralty has driven a coal boom unmatched anywhere in the eastern duchy. New pithead structures, company terraces, and barge stations appear faster than the [[Commons' Assembly]] can regulate them.

The town's prosperity is real but uneven. Wages are paid on time; conditions deteriorate steadily; the ridge itself is increasingly pockmarked with new shafts. Outwardly, Redmarsh is a model of frontier industry. Beneath the surface, its growth is sustained by the secret extraction of [[Wondrous Coal]] and the quiet partnership between [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]] and the **[[Glasswrights' Covenant]]**.

##### **[[Grayfen Crossing]]**

**Population:** ~6,500  
**Position:** Border between the [[Helwing Lowlands]] and the northern Fenward Marches, where the fen-rivers narrow at a controlled crossing  
**Role:** Toll collection, ferries, patrol mustering, frontier defence

Properly speaking, [[Grayfen Crossing]] sits on the Lowlands–Marches boundary rather than fully within the Marches themselves — a distinction the town's residents take more seriously than anyone else. Founded as the duchy's northern bulwark in an age when everything beyond was untamed, the town has spent its entire history as a chokepoint, and the original frontier function has now reasserted itself with awkward urgency.

Grayfen controls one of the few reliable routes through the northern marshes. Its fortifications are old but maintained; its garrison is the largest organised force in the eastern duchy; and its toll-gates are now the principal point of contact between the duchy and the refugees descending from the highlands. Most refugees who pass through Grayfen are routed onward toward [[Highford]] and the inner Lowlands. A significant minority — those refused passage, those without the means, those who hear about Redmarsh's hiring needs from the wrong sort of recruiter — are pressed instead into the Marches.

#### **Lesser Settlements**

The Marches contain a number of smaller communities, most of them strung along travel routes or clustered on minor ridges. Four are of particular note to travellers and patrols:

- **Kelter's Ford** — A modest ferry settlement on the Redmarsh tributary, roughly halfway between [[Redmarsh]] and the Highwater confluence. Kelter's Ford serves the barge traffic moving Redmarsh coal westward, and operates a seasonal market in late summer when the fens are dry enough to bring livestock through. The town reeve is paid partly by the duchy and partly by whichever barge-master most recently bribed her, and is generally honest about both.
    
- **Otterbarrow** — A ridge-top agricultural community on the overland causeway between [[Redmarsh]] and [[Brackenmere]]. The only reliable supply stop on that long, miserable journey, Otterbarrow grows hardy grains and runs a flock of marsh-cattle whose meat is famously tough and famously cheap. Travellers heading to Brackenmere generally aim to arrive at Otterbarrow before nightfall; those who fail learn why.
    
- **Brinefell** — A small fishing settlement near the silted mouth of the Brackenmere river, where the freshwater channels give way to brackish tidal flats. Brinefell harvests reed-fish, eels, waterfowl, and small quantities of salt cut from the coastal pans, and supplies most of [[Brackenmere]]'s preserved goods. It is one of the few settlements in the Marches with any meaningful relationship to the open water — though that relationship is mostly nets, weirs, and shallow skiffs rather than anything resembling proper sea trade. Brinefell shares Brackenmere's isolation and most of its problems.
    
- **Low Fenmead** — A scattered hamlet of peat-cutters and reed gatherers deep in the southern fens, reachable only by barge during the wet months and by careful walking during the dry. Low Fenmead exports peat westward via Kelter's Ford, and is best known for the fact that nobody who lives there agrees on where, exactly, the hamlet's boundaries lie.
    

Beyond these, the Marches contain perhaps two dozen smaller hamlets, peat camps, fishing stations, salt-pans, and barge moorings, most of them too transient or too small to appear on ducal records. A handful of these sit directly on the coast — quiet, weather-beaten places of a few dozen souls each, where the duchy's writ runs thin and ducal taxes thinner.

#### **Travel and Trade Routes**

Movement through the Marches falls into three broad networks.

The **western corridor** is the principal route of contact with the rest of the duchy. It runs north–south along the Lowlands–Marches boundary, linking [[Highford]] to [[Stonecross]] and [[Grayfen Crossing]], with branches east into the fens. This is the route most refugees travel, most ducal couriers prefer, and most patrols defend.

The **river network** carries the majority of bulk goods. Coal and peat move west along the Redmarsh and Grayfen tributaries to the Highwater, and from there downstream to [[Willowreach]] and beyond. River barges are the cheapest reliable transport in the duchy, but they are slow, weather-dependent, and increasingly subject to inspection at Redmarsh and Grayfen alike. The Brackenmere river system is part of no useful network.

The **eastern overland routes** connect the inner Marches — most importantly the long, weary causeway between [[Redmarsh]] and [[Brackenmere]] via Otterbarrow, and a thinner, less-used track running from Brackenmere down to Brinefell and the coast. These routes are slower than rivers, costlier than rivers, and entirely the only option for reaching [[Brackenmere]] from the rest of the duchy. They are also more vulnerable: a flooded causeway can isolate a settlement for weeks, and bandits, displaced soldiers, and worse have all made use of the eastern roads in the past year.

#### **Population**

**Estimated total population:** ~22,000–26,000 (excluding refugees in transit)

The Marches' population is unevenly distributed. [[Grayfen Crossing]] alone accounts for roughly a quarter of the regional total, with [[Brackenmere]] another fifth and [[Redmarsh]] growing rapidly toward similar numbers. The remainder is scattered across the lesser settlements, hamlets, peat camps, and isolated coastal communities.

The Marches are notably more **mixed-ancestry** than the western Lowlands, with a long-standing dwarven mining presence (particularly around Redmarsh), a substantial human and half-elven majority, and significant numbers of orcs, half-orcs, and halflings drawn by either fen agriculture or industrial wages. The marsh elves of the deep fens keep largely to themselves but trade with Brinefell and Low Fenmead.

##### **Refugees**

The conclusion of the northern war has pushed perhaps three to five thousand displaced people into the Marches, with more arriving each month. The majority pass through [[Grayfen Crossing]] aiming for [[Highford]] and the wider Lowlands, where shelter and work are easier to find. A growing minority remain — turned back at Grayfen for lack of papers or means, redirected toward Redmarsh by recruiters acting on [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s behalf, or simply too tired to go further. Refugee camps have sprung up around Grayfen, on the outskirts of Redmarsh, and along the western corridor as far south as Stonecross. Tensions between refugees and established communities are rising but have not yet produced serious violence.

#### **Economy**

##### **Coal and Peat**

The defining industries of the Marches. Ordinary coal from Redmarsh's upper seams supplies the duchy's forges, kilns, and increasingly its winter hearths. Peat from the wider fens — cut at Brackenmere, Low Fenmead, and dozens of smaller camps — fuels the rural economy and is exported westward in quantity. Behind both, hidden from the [[Commons' Assembly]] and the wider public, the [[Wondrous Coal]] trade quietly funds [[Ephraim Gauge|Gauge]]'s ambitions and the [[Glasswrights' Covenant|Glasswrights']] research.

##### **River Trade**

The Highwater tributaries carry coal, peat, ore, grain, and timber out of the Marches and bring back tools, cloth, salt, and finished goods. Barge families form a tight, hereditary trade community with their own conventions, grievances, and informal hierarchy. They are a useful resource for travellers willing to pay and behave themselves.

##### **Fen and Coast**

Hardy grains, reed crops, fish stocks, waterfowl, marsh-cattle, and modest quantities of salt cut from the coastal pans. Productive enough to sustain the region most years, vulnerable to flooding in bad ones. Almost no surplus is exported. What coastal trade exists is small-scale, local, and often unrecorded — small craft moving fish, salt, and the occasional unlicensed cargo between coastal hamlets without ever passing through a customs post.

##### **Stagnant and Hidden Economies**

[[Brackenmere]]'s economy has barely grown in two generations, and its share of regional trade declines further each year. Conversely, Redmarsh's true economy is substantially larger than its public accounts suggest, with the difference accounted for in unrecorded shipments to the [[Glasswrights' Covenant]].

#### **Governance**

The Marches fall under the nominal authority of the Duke of Marlowe-Helwing, but distance, terrain, and the duke's incapacitation have left local leaders to their own devices. Most settlements are run by some combination of:

- **Town reeves**, often hereditary or near-hereditary
- **Mining councils**, where applicable
- **Commons' assemblies**, of which Redmarsh's is the most formalised
- **Barge wardens**, who police the rivers and collect informal tolls
- **Fen-wardens**, who manage causeway maintenance and seasonal access

Politics across the region favour pragmatism, consensus where possible, and stubborn negotiation otherwise. Ducal officials are tolerated; ducal _writs_ are obeyed selectively. [[Redmarsh]] currently exerts the strongest political influence in the region thanks to its growing population, industrial output, and the assertive — and increasingly authoritarian — leadership of [[Ephraim Gauge|Mayor Gauge]].

The coast is barely governed at all. No reeve formally administers the coastal hamlets as a group; each settlement answers, in theory, to whichever inland town it lies nearest, and in practice to nobody.

#### **Current Pressures**

Five overlapping pressures shape the Marches at the start of the campaign:

1. **The end of the northern war** has produced a steady inflow of refugees, with no clear plan for absorbing them and no functional ducal authority to direct one.
2. **The Iron Host's proximity** has not yet produced an invasion, but it has produced fear, rumour, and a steady recalculation of how defensible the Marches actually are.
3. **Grayfen Crossing's renewed military function** has shifted a town that had begun to slide toward customs-and-tolls back toward serious garrison duty, with all the political weight that implies.
4. **Redmarsh's industrial growth** is straining the Marches' labour markets, refugee tolerance, and political balance, and concealing a far more dangerous resource trade beneath its public ledgers.
5. **Brackenmere's slow decline** has produced a large, frustrated population in an isolated town with reasons to resent both the duchy and its more prosperous neighbours — a condition that historically does not stay quiet forever.

Any of these pressures alone would be manageable. Together, they make the Marches the most volatile region of the duchy.
