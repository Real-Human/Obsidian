**Type:** Foreign military power 
**Endonym:** Thurun-Kadresh-Ai 
**Leader:** [[Oskuresh]]
**Territory:** The northern kingdoms, conquered; northern passes held 
**Status:** Not at war with [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing|Marlowe-Helwing]]. Not at peace.

#### **Overview**

The **Iron Host** is the army that broke the north. In four years it destroyed the Allied Armies of the Northern March Kingdoms, took the highland passes from the Duchy of Highcrest, annihilated the Royal Host of Karrowen in a single afternoon, and reduced the Eastern Fenward Freeholds to a memory carried by refugees. Cities that refused terms were taken with their garrisons inside them.

It has not entered [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing|Marlowe-Helwing]]. No declaration has been made, no envoy sent, no border crossed in force. The duchy's court regards this as evidence that no war is coming.

The court is wrong, and the reason is not strategic. The Iron Host is coming south because its commander cannot stop it.

#### **The Name**

**Iron Host** is not their name. It is the duchy's name for them, and it is older than the war — it is the phrase that appears in two centuries of contracts, ledgers and paymasters' returns. _Hired, the Iron Host, four hundred spears, paid in full._ Nobody in Marlowe-Helwing chose it as a title. It is simply the entry.

The word _host_ is ordinary in-world usage for an army — compare the Royal Host of Karrowen — so to duchy ears the phrase is not a proper name at all but a description. _That army. The iron one._ Nobody learned what they called themselves, because one does not learn the name of a tool.

Their own name is **Thurun-Kadresh-Ai**: _those who hold the line unbroken._

##### **The clipping**

Duchy speakers invariably shorten this to **the Thurun**. In their own tongue the construction does not survive the loss.

- **Thurun** — the agent. Those who hold.
- **Kadresh** — the object. The line.
- **Ai** — completion. Unbroken, without end.

Strip the last two and what remains is a bare agent noun with nothing attached. In their grammar this is not an incomplete phrase but a specific and formal one: the construction used for a person whose function has lapsed. A widow. A discharged soldier. A guild member struck from the roll.

_The Thurun_ therefore does not land as a nickname. It lands as the formal term for people who used to have a purpose.

No one in Marlowe-Helwing has ever meant it as an insult. It has been one every time.

##### **Who says what**

They will not correct it. Asking for courtesy is beneath them, so it passes at every parley, every negotiation, every set of terms — and costs something each time. A party who learns this and uses the full form at a table has done something no ducal factor has ever done, and it will be noticed by people who show no sign of noticing anything.

_Ai_ also stands alone. It is the closing word of an oath, the last word of a treaty, and the word cut into a grave marker. When an officer of the Thurun-Kadresh-Ai accepts terms and says _Ai_, that is how binding it is.

#### **Origins**

They have always been soldiers. Not conscripts and not a warrior caste in the ornamental sense — a people culturally and physically suited to holding a formation in heavy armour under sustained pressure, and hired for exactly that by every power within reach for as long as anyone has kept records.

This was not resented for most of that history. It was simply the work. The Thurun-Kadresh-Ai were reliable, expensive, scrupulous about terms, and consistently the most competent heavy infantry on any field they were paid to stand on.

What they were never permitted to be was a country. Employers do not want a nation of soldiers; they want a supply of them. Companies that stayed together too long were broken up by the terms of their next contract. Land granted in payment was granted to individuals, never to the people. Two centuries of this produced a scattered, capable, permanently temporary population — mercenaries where the pay was good, bandits where it was not, and in the eyes of every court that used them, interchangeable.

#### **Oskuresh**

**[[Oskuresh]]** is the commander who ended that. His achievement was not tactical — the Thurun-Kadresh-Ai have always won battles — but political: he was the first to hold enough companies together long enough to take and keep territory, and he did it by refusing every contract offered to him during the two years it took.

He has taken no royal title. In their naming convention a personal name is followed by a service-mark recording the contract one was born under; Oskuresh refused his, and is therefore known by the bare form alone — the same lapsed-function construction the duchy uses without knowing it. He carries the insult in his own name on purpose.

Duchy speakers clip him to **Oskur**. He has never remarked on it.

##### **His bind**

Oskuresh conquered the north to give his people somewhere to stop. He wanted a country: settlement, charter, tenure, an end to the ledger entry.

His army does not want a country. They want the next war.

Two centuries of being hired, disbanded and re-hired have produced a people who understand themselves through campaigning and have no idea what they are when they are not doing it. Offered a kingdom to live in, they experienced it as being stood down — which, historically, is what happens immediately before they are dispersed and forgotten. What Oskuresh reads as victory, his army reads as the beginning of the end, and their instinct is not wrong.

Companies that grew restive drifted off to raid and never came back. Some went north. A significant number came **south**, into Marlowe-Helwing.

So he is marching. Not because he wants the duchy, but because a moving army stays an army, and a stationary one becomes what it was before him. He is aware of the shape of this. He has calculated, correctly, that he cannot win his people a homeland without a war that will prevent them ever settling in it, and he is proceeding anyway, because the alternative is watching them dissolve back into other men's contracts within a generation.

He would stop tomorrow if stopping did not dissolve them.

##### **Consequences at the table**

- **Negotiation fails without him being unreasonable.** He will agree with a well-argued case for peace. He will not be able to act on it. Parties expecting a villain to out-argue will find something worse.
- **Assassination is counterproductive and the party must work this out.** Remove Oskuresh and the Iron Host does not collapse — it fragments into precisely the roaming bands already bleeding into the fens, all at once, with nobody to answer to and nowhere to be recalled from.
- **Military victory is available, and insufficient.** This is the important one. Let the players win the field battle if they earn it. The result is Oskuresh dead or discredited, twelve thousand leaderless veterans dispersing into a duchy that has just spent its army holding them off, and the fens permanently full of remnant companies with no command structure left to recall them. The duchy wins the war and is dismantled over the following decade. Same lesson as the assassination trap, learned the hard way and at full scale — and it means the theme survives the players winning, so no battle ever needs fudging to protect it.
- **The only durable ending is solving his problem for him.** Anything that gives his people a purpose that is not war, or a way to become a nation without ceasing to be themselves, is a route Act 3 can actually take. This is deliberately difficult and should not be signposted.

#### **The Atrocities**

Cities were annihilated with their garrisons inside. This was his army's excess, not his policy, and his loss of control began during the conquest rather than after it — the northern kingdom he won is already half-wrecked by the people he won it for, which is one more reason none of them wish to stay in it.

He has never publicly disavowed any of it. Doing so would admit he does not command.

#### **Military Character**

- **Heavy line infantry.** Full armour, close order, sustained engagement. They are built and trained to hold a line under conditions that break other formations.
- **Decisive in the open field.** Karrowen's Royal Host was destroyed in one battle. This is the standard outcome, not an exceptional one.
- **Competent besiegers.** Multiple cities taken. They are not a raiding horde that fails against walls.
- **They hold ground afterwards.** Conquered territory is garrisoned, not abandoned.
- **Extensive scouting.** Scouts and raiding parties operate far ahead of the main force and have made repeated contact with the [[The Displaced Companies|Displaced Companies]].

##### **Strength, and why the ratio is not the point**

Some **18,000–22,000** under arms in the north, of which perhaps **12,000–15,000** can be brought south while holding conquered territory.

This is deliberately thin. Against fortifications a commander would want three to one; Oskuresh has closer to one and a half, and only if the duchy concentrates. He is not marching because the odds are good. He is marching because he has no choice, and the arithmetic should reflect that.

The duchy's ~9,000–11,000 is a **paper figure and should never be treated as available**:

|Component|Nominal|Reality|
|---|---|---|
|Standing ducal troops|3,500|Answer to [[Helwing Hold]], where the Duke is incapacitated and nobody can lawfully issue the order|
|Town militias|4,000|Defend their own towns. [[Redmarsh]]'s will not march to [[Stonecross]]|
|River wardens & patrols|1,500|Dispersed by function; cannot mass without abandoning the waterways|
|Noble retainers & private forces|1,000|Answer to patrons, and patrons are waiting to see who wins|

A duchy that does no political work concentrates perhaps **4,000–5,000**. Against 12,000–15,000 of the finest heavy infantry on the continent, that is not a battle.

**This is what Act 2 is for.** The party are not adding strength to the duchy — they are unlocking strength that already exists on paper and currently cannot be moved. The mandate from [[Prince Corvin]], the internal alliances, the succession problem, the question of who can lawfully command: every one of those converts nominal troops into real ones. A party that does Act 2 well brings 11,000 to the field. A party that does it badly brings 5,000 to the same war, and can feel the difference.

##### **On force multipliers**

Spellcasters, artificers and heavy cavalry are worth far more than their headcount, and the alliance-gathering of Act 2 should reflect that. Two things keep it bounded without nerfing anything:

- **The Host has fought casters before.** They have destroyed multiple kingdoms, several of which fielded magic and knights. They have doctrine for both: dispersed approach where possible, armour, and a standing priority on killing casters first. This is realistic rather than punitive, and it means magic changes the shape of an engagement rather than deciding it in advance.
- **Glasswright weapons are a consumable, not an armoury.** No combat-grade work exists at campaign start, and Burn-Cells give three to five discharges per fragment before exhaustion. Wonder-weapons are therefore rationed by the output of [[Redmarsh]]'s deep seams, which makes [[Wondrous Coal]] a strategic variable in Act 3 and drags the endgame back to the town where the campaign began.

#### **Conduct**

They observe the forms of war with total precision. Heralds are sent properly. Terms are offered before assault and are exactly what they say. Prisoners are handled correctly. Agreements are honoured to the letter — and to nothing whatever beyond it.

This is not decency. It is a claim to legitimacy by a people whose entire grievance is being treated as hired thugs, and it is the only argument available to them that a court might hear. Their punctiliousness is the case for their nationhood, made in the only language courts respect.

The horror of them is the gap between how the Host conducts itself and what its unleashed remnants do in the fens. The duchy finds this gap incomprehensible and concludes that the courtesy is a deception. It is not.

**Register at the table:** formal, correct, unhurried. No boasting, no threats, no rhetoric. An officer states terms, states the consequence of refusal as a matter of record, and waits. Nothing is said twice.

#### **The Remnants**

Companies that broke away during and after the northern conquest. They are in the duchy **now**, and have been for over a year.

- They are bandits and they behave like bandits. Raids on barge traffic, outlying farms, refugee columns, isolated holdings.
- They retain their training, which makes them far more dangerous than the label suggests. A dozen of them will destroy a militia patrol.
- They observe none of the Host's forms. No terms, no heralds, no restraint.
- They are not under orders and cannot be negotiated with as a faction, because they are not one.
- Ancestry does not identify them. Hobgoblins have been part of this world's mercenary and freebooting economy for centuries and are ordinary residents, hauliers, veterans and second-generation locals throughout the duchy. A hobgoblin in [[Redmarsh]] is unremarkable. What has changed is that some of them now have somewhere to be loyal to.

This is why there is Iron Host violence in the duchy before any declaration of war, and why nobody escalates over it. Bandits are bandits. This is precisely how a war arrives while everyone insists it has not.

#### **The Northern Holdouts**

Fighting continues in the far north against the last unconquered holdouts. This is the single most important fact about the current situation and almost nobody in the duchy understands its significance.

While the holdouts stand, Oskuresh has somewhere to point his army. They are the pressure valve keeping his people occupied and his authority intact. When the last of them falls he will have nothing left to give, and marching south stops being a decision.

**The thing the duchy is quietly hoping for is the thing that dooms it.**

This is the trigger for Act 2. It is GM-timed.

It also gives [[Ephraim Gauge]] a rational basis for a certainty he could never justify at court: he is not tracking troop movements, he is tracking **how much fighting is left in the north**. He has been buying that information for years. It is the one subject on which he is entirely honest with himself and entirely unable to be honest with anyone else.

#### **Approach**

Read against the map.

##### **The northern notch**

The northern range runs unbroken from the eastern coast westward, then breaks — a clear gap at the centre-north — before resuming as a separate western range. The main river descends south through that gap, and below it the ground opens directly into the Helwing Lowlands and the north–south road.

This is the invasion route. There is no other that will carry a supply train.

##### **Grayfen Crossing**

[[Grayfen Crossing]] sits square on the notch's eastern shoulder. This is why it is fortified out of all proportion to its trade and why it musters patrols rather than merely collecting tolls. It is the cork.

[[Stonecross]] is the second line, south-west at the river bridge.

##### **The western flank**

The western range stops well short of the border, leaving unbarred country above the Western Ridges. This is not the oversight it appears: the ground there is dense forest and broken hill — passable for scouts and raiding parties, ruinous for anything with a baggage train.

It is a raiding flank, not an invasion route. Expect remnants and scouts, not columns.

##### **The Marches**

The eastern range runs unbroken to the coast. There is no northern land approach to [[Brackenmere]] or [[Redmarsh]]. The Marches are genuinely screened, and the duchy's assumption that its eastern settlements are insulated is correct.

_As far as it goes._ See below.

#### **Open Threads**

##### **The Eastern Fenward Freeholds**

The Fenward Exiles among the [[The Displaced Companies|Displaced Companies]] were fen-bound city militias and river guards from the **Eastern Fenward Freeholds** — fen country, already fallen to the Host. But the map places unbroken mountain north of the Marches and open water east.

So either those Freeholds lay beyond the eastern range, or they lay **across the water**.

If the latter, the Host has fen country and a coastline on the far side of that sea, and the Marches are not screened at all — they are facing the wrong way, and [[Redmarsh]] is not the safe rear area everyone including Gauge believes it to be.

This depends on the undecided question of what the eastern sea is, and should be resolved before Act 2.
