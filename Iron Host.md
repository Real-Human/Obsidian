**Type:** Foreign military power 
**Endonym:** Thurun-Kadresh-Ai 
**Leader:** [[Oskuresh]]
**Territory:** The northern kingdoms, conquered; northern passes held 
**Status:** Not at war with [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing|Marlowe-Helwing]]. Not at peace.

#### **Overview**

The **Iron Host** is the army that conquered the northern realms over the course of four years. In that period it destroyed the Allied Armies of the Northern March Kingdoms, took the highland passes from the Duchy of Highcrest, annihilated the Royal Host of Karrowen in a single engagement, and reduced the Eastern Fenward Freeholds to a population in exile. Cities that refused terms were taken with their garrisons inside them.

It has not entered the [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing]]. No declaration has been issued, no envoy sent, and no border crossed in force. Fighting continues in the far north against the last unconquered holdouts.

#### **The Name**

**Iron Host** is an exonym. It does not originate as a title but as a ledger entry: the phrase appears in two centuries of contracts, muster returns and paymasters' accounts throughout the southern realms. _Hired, the Iron Host, four hundred spears, paid in full._

The word _host_ is ordinary usage for an army — compare the Royal Host of Karrowen — so to southern ears the phrase reads as description rather than name. That army. The iron one.

Their own name for themselves is **Thurun-Kadresh-Ai**: _those who hold the line unbroken._

##### **Structure and the clipped form**

Speakers in Marlowe-Helwing almost universally shorten this to **the Thurun**. The construction does not survive the loss.

- **Thurun** — the agent. Those who hold.
- **Kadresh** — the object. The line.
- **Ai** — completion. Unbroken, without end.

Removing the final two elements leaves a bare agent noun with no object attached. In the Thurun-Kadresh-Ai tongue this is not an incomplete phrase but a specific and formal one: the construction used of a person whose function has lapsed. A widow. A discharged soldier. A guild member struck from the roll.

_The Thurun_ therefore does not read as an abbreviation. It reads as the formal term for people who once had a purpose.

The usage is not known to be deliberate on the part of any southern speaker. It has never been corrected by any Thurun-Kadresh-Ai officer, on the grounds that to request courtesy is to concede one is not owed it.

##### **Ai**

The element stands alone in several formal contexts. It is the closing word of an oath, the final word of a treaty, and the word cut into a grave marker. An officer who accepts terms and says _Ai_ has bound the agreement absolutely.

##### **Pronunciation**

THOO-run ka-DRESH-eye. The final element is a single diphthong. Southern speakers attempting the full form typically insert a hard stop before it.

#### **Origins**

The Thurun-Kadresh-Ai have been professional soldiers for as long as records exist. They are culturally and physically suited to holding a formation in heavy armour under sustained pressure, and were hired for that purpose by every power within reach for the better part of two centuries.

This arrangement was not, for most of its history, a grievance. The Thurun-Kadresh-Ai were reliable, expensive, exacting about the terms of a contract, and consistently the most effective heavy infantry available for hire.

What they were never permitted to become was a nation. Employers required a supply of soldiers rather than a country of them. Companies that remained together too long were separated by the terms of their subsequent contracts. Land granted in payment was granted to individuals and never to the people. The cumulative result was a capable, scattered and permanently provisional population: mercenaries where the pay was reliable, freebooters where it was not, and interchangeable in the estimation of every court that employed them.

#### **Oskuresh**

The northern conquest was achieved under **[[Oskuresh]]**, the first commander to hold sufficient companies together long enough to take and retain territory. He did so by refusing every contract offered during the two years the consolidation required.

The Host's continued southward movement is generally attributed to appetite for conquest. It is more accurately attributed to the fact that its commander is unable to halt it: an army that stops campaigning ceases, by the only definition its members recognise, to exist. See [[Oskuresh]].

#### **Military Character**

- **Heavy line infantry.** Full armour, close order, sustained engagement. Trained and built to hold formation under conditions that break other troops.
- **Decisive in the open field.** The destruction of Karrowen's Royal Host in one engagement is characteristic rather than exceptional.
- **Competent in siege.** Multiple cities taken. They are not a force that fails against walls.
- **They garrison what they take.** Conquered territory is held, not abandoned.
- **Extensive forward scouting.** Scouts and raiding parties operate well ahead of the main body and have made repeated contact with the [[The Displaced Companies|Displaced Companies]].

##### **Doctrine against magic**

Several of the realms destroyed during the northern campaign fielded spellcasters and heavy cavalry. The Host has established practice against both: dispersed approach where ground permits, heavy armour, and a standing priority on identifying and killing casters before an engagement closes.

##### **Estimated strength**

Some 18,000 to 22,000 under arms in the north. Of these, perhaps 12,000 to 15,000 could be committed southward while the conquered territories remained garrisoned.

By conventional measure this is a thin margin for an invading force, particularly against fortified positions.

#### **Conduct**

The Host observes the forms of war with precision. Heralds are sent correctly. Terms are offered in advance of assault and are exactly as stated. Prisoners are handled according to established practice. Agreements are honoured to the letter, and to nothing beyond it.

This is not sentiment. It is a claim to legitimacy advanced by a people whose grievance is that they have been treated as hired labour, and it is the only such claim a court is equipped to hear. Their punctiliousness is the argument for their nationhood, made in the register that courts respect.

The disparity between the conduct of the Host proper and that of its detached remnants is considerable, and is widely interpreted in the south as evidence that the formality is a deception.

##### **Register**

Formal, correct, unhurried. Without boast, threat or rhetoric. An officer states terms, states the consequence of refusal as a matter of record, and waits. Nothing is repeated.

#### **The Remnants**

Companies that separated from the main body during and after the northern conquest. A significant number are within the [[Duchy of Marlowe-Helwing]] and have been for over a year.

- They conduct themselves as bandits: raids on barge traffic, outlying farms, refugee columns and isolated holdings.
- They retain their training, which makes them substantially more dangerous than the description suggests. A dozen will destroy a militia patrol.
- They observe none of the Host's forms — no terms, no heralds, no restraint.
- They are not under command and do not constitute a faction with which anything can be negotiated.

Ancestry does not identify them. Hobgoblins have formed part of the mercenary and freebooting economy of the southern realms for centuries and are found throughout the duchy as residents, hauliers, veterans and second-generation locals. A hobgoblin in [[Redmarsh]] is unremarkable.

Because the remnants are indistinguishable from ordinary banditry, and are treated as such by ducal authorities, no incident involving them has been escalated as an act of war.

#### **Territory and Approach**

##### **The northern range**

The mountain range along the duchy's northern boundary runs unbroken from the eastern coast westward before breaking at a gap near the centre-north, resuming beyond it as a separate western range. The principal river descends south through this gap, below which the ground opens directly into the Helwing Lowlands and the north-south road.

This is the only approach that will carry a supply train.

##### **Grayfen Crossing**

[[Grayfen Crossing]] stands on the eastern shoulder of the gap. This accounts for its fortification, which is disproportionate to its trade, and for its function as a mustering point rather than merely a toll station. [[Stonecross]] stands as the second position, south-west at the river bridge.

##### **The western flank**

The western range terminates well short of the duchy's western boundary, leaving unbarred country above the Western Ridges. The ground there is dense forest and broken hill: passable to scouts and raiding parties, impracticable for a force with a baggage train.

##### **The Fenward Marches**

The eastern range runs unbroken to the coast. There is no northern land approach to [[Brackenmere]] or [[Redmarsh]], and the eastern settlements of the duchy are, by land, screened.

The Eastern Fenward Freeholds, whose surviving militias now serve among the [[The Displaced Companies|Displaced Companies]], were fen country and have fallen to the Host. Their position relative to the eastern range and the eastern water is not established in surviving records.
